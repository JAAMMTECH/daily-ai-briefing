### NariQ: BF16-level Qwen3.8-27B in a 10 GB file

Hacker News

Nari Labs released 3-bit, 2.5-bit, and 2-bit GGUFs of Qwen3.8-27B that run on its own llama.cpp fork, and the post describes a near-BF16 model in a 10 GB file. On its runs, DeepSWE scored 39.8 for BF16, 41.0 at 3-bit, 41.9 at 2.5-bit, and 28.6 at 2-bit; Terminal-Bench 2.1 scored 62.6, 63.8, 60.2, and 52.4.

Why it's worth knowing: The 3-bit and 2.5-bit files hold the BF16 line on long agent tasks, math, and images, while the 2-bit file drops, so the size cut is not free at every rate. AIME 25+26 followed the same pattern at 97.7, 98.3, 96.5, and 86.0, and the files will not load in stock llama.cpp.

Terms:
- **BF16** — brain floating point 16 — A 16-bit number format used as the full-precision reference. NariQ's claim is that the 3-bit and 2.5-bit files still score like that reference on the tests they published.

### AutoPILOT: A Visual Agent Harness for Real-Time Gameplay

Hugging Face papers

AutoPILOT lets a gameplay model, in one call, send a single action, start an open-loop macro, or write a closed-loop controller that keeps playing from the screen until it hands control back. With GPT-6.1 Sol on AFK-Bench's 120 browser games, real-time success was 68.6% against 38.3% for VISTA, and on GameWorld it held 80.0% while VISTA fell from 84.1% to 42.9%.

Why it's worth knowing: A model that plays well when the game waits can still miss when the clock keeps running, and the measured fix is in the harness rather than a new model. The same setup reached VISTA's final success rate at about a fifth of the API cost.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28202

### Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness

Hugging Face papers

Embo-Harness uses a frontier multimodal model directly as a robot policy, and its recipe prefers the model's own visual reasoning and tool use over hand-built plans or action priors. On RoboLab-120 it reached 83.3% success, 26.2 percentage points above the strongest reported baseline, and it succeeded in 15 of 20 zero-shot trials across four real-world manipulation tasks.

Why it's worth knowing: The lab gain says the loop around a general model can move a manipulation score without a specialized robot policy. The real-world sample is 20 trials, so the 83.3% simulation figure is the firmer number.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31201

### Let the Fixer Decide: Training a Code Localizer from the Fixer's Likelihood

Hugging Face papers

Fixer-Guided Localization trains a code localizer on a frozen fixer's rating of how likely each file set makes a verified patch, with no test runs and no reinforcement learning. On a Gemma 4 E4B agent built from 1,629 verified trajectories, that target matched the teacher's bug-finding rate while reading 27.5% less code, and one trained agent solved up to 11 of 78 tasks against 8 for the base model.

Why it's worth knowing: The files a patch edits are not always the files a fixer needs to read, so copying the patch's file list is the wrong training target. One development round also got more tasks to a patch, 53 versus 46, and fewer stuck on files that do not exist, 43 versus 54.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12164

### ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step

Hugging Face papers

ViDecide trains a video diffusion model to predict the next state and decide when to stop, instead of treating a plan as one fixed-length clip. Across abstract games, planning tasks, and multi-step robot planning, it reports an average strict success rate of 83.8%, against 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior method.

Why it's worth knowing: The gap over ordinary fine-tuning is the result that matters: more reasoning data alone had not stopped these models from breaking physics and logic across several steps. Corrective fine-tuning maps a failed decision's noise onto the model's own successful continuation, without a policy-gradient update.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26389

### A Glimpse of Training VLMs as Foundation Models for Robotic Manipulation

Hugging Face papers

nanoAstra fine-tunes a vision-language model for manipulation by writing behavior as reusable action steps plus progress notes, and by pinning outputs to a robot-specific prompt. Trained on more than 5,000 hours of robot data plus general visual questions, the 9B model scored 17.8% zero-shot on RoboTwin 2.0's randomized benchmark, ahead of the vision-language-action and world-action baselines in the abstract, with inference faster than the motion on one H20 GPU.

Why it's worth knowing: A 17.8% success rate is still low, and it is a measured zero-shot number for a 9B model on a randomized simulation benchmark rather than a demo. The authors present it as a training recipe aimed at the kind of robot generalization GPT-6 Astra has shown, not as a product launch.

Terms:
- **VLM** — vision-language model — A model that takes images and text together. nanoAstra adapts one to robot control with a structured action prompt instead of training a separate action model from scratch.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29338

### WeMM-Ecom: Advancing Multimodal Content Understanding for WeChat E-Commerce

Hugging Face papers

WeChat's WeMM-Ecom family runs from a 0.8B dense model to a 35B mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters, after extra e-commerce pretraining and a hybrid thinking stage. On 28 e-commerce benchmarks the 35B-A3B model surpassed larger open models including Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, HY4, and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and every size beat a similarly sized Qwen3.5 on 64 general benchmarks.

Why it's worth knowing: A model with about 3 billion active parameters ranking above much larger general models on a shopping suite is a domain-data result, not a scale result. The abstract gives the ranking and does not include the score margin.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30662

### BCE Sees Bigger AI Infrastructure Opportunity While Targeting Debt Reduction

MarketBeat

BCE says Bell AI Fabric is past the 73 megawatts in its investor-day plan: 335 megawatts are contracted and 373 are in the plan, with line of sight to 1.2 gigawatts in Saskatchewan. A further 900-megawatt Saskatchewan case still needs customer contracts, land, and power before the company commits capital or sets a timeline.

Why it's worth knowing: The contracted figure is more than four times the capacity BCE had been willing to put in its three-year model. The 900-megawatt project is still a prospect, and BCE said any returns have to compete with other uses of capital, including fibre.

### P.E.I. premier 'not interested' in AI data centre as Canadian communities weigh the pros, cons

CBC News

Prince Edward Island Premier Rob Lantz told the legislature the province has not been approached to host a large AI data centre and that the answer is no, citing resource use and the economic return. AI Minister Zack Bell said the province still wants businesses to adopt AI for productivity, set against land, water, and electricity.

Why it's worth knowing: P.E.I. has a small grid and limited land, so a premier's refusal is a planning decision even without an application on the table. The same CBC report separates that refusal from ordinary business use of AI, which the province says it still encourages.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/prince-edward-island/pei-ai-premier-not-interested-communities-9.7433229

### Jet.AI Announces StratGrid's Proposed Wheatland AI Campus

GlobeNewswire

StratGrid described a proposed 240 MW off-grid campus in Wheatland County, Alberta, and Jet.AI said it holds about a 15% economic interest. The concept uses closed-loop cooling with dry coolers, air-cooled condensers, and non-potable industrial water, with surveys underway for an Alberta Utilities Commission application and an open house intended for early 2027.

Why it's worth knowing: The design is an attempt to answer water and cooling objections before a regulator filing, using industrial raw water and dry coolers rather than evaporative towers. It is still subject to final engineering, and it is not an approved campus.

### BDC to manage Canada’s $100-million contribution to NATO Innovation Fund

BetaKit

The Business Development Bank of Canada will manage Canada's $100 million contribution to the NATO Innovation Fund. BetaKit describes the move as another route for Canadian defence-technology companies to find customers and capital.

Why it's worth knowing: Naming a manager and a dollar figure turns a defence-innovation commitment into something companies can try to use. The report does not say when the first Canadian firm would receive money, or which technologies the fund will prefer.

- **NATO Innovation Fund** — North Atlantic Treaty Organization Innovation Fund — A multi-country fund for defence and security technology. This announcement assigns management of Canada's contribution to BDC.
