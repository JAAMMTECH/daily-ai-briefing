### Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Orchestrator

Hacker News

Google is rolling out Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Antigravity, plus an Orchestrator that moves a single task between Hydrogen and a stronger model such as Gemini 4 Carbon. On Terminal-Bench 4.1, Google says that mix stayed near Carbon's success rate while using 24 percent less quota and finishing 19 percent faster.

Why it's worth knowing: Agent runs get expensive when every step uses the strongest model. A handoff inside one session is a concrete way to spend frontier quota on the steps that need it.

https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-in-google-antigravity/
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472260

### Edge0-145B: Frontier AI Capabilities on Your Own Computer

Hacker News

Edge0 has open-sourced Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B with its inference stack, and says the 145-billion-parameter model runs on a laptop at more than 80 tokens per second with about 28 GB of peak memory. In its design, each group of 64 experts shares two large matrices, and each expert keeps a small set of parameters of its own.

Why it's worth knowing: Memory, not the parameter count on a spec sheet, is what keeps large models off personal computers. Shared expert matrices are one attempt to cut the weights that have to move for every token.

https://edge0.ai/blog/introducing-edge0-145b-frontier-ai-capabilities-on-your-own-computer
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50475713

### I'm making LLMs work on small and old devices

Hacker News

smolmaxxing is a llama.cpp patch that keeps only the experts in use in memory and reads the rest from flash. Measured on the hardware, a TV box and a jailbroken iPad run 8B and 16B mixture-of-experts models on about 1 GB of RAM, and a phone runs 35B and 80B models with about 3 GB free.

Why it's worth knowing: Local inference usually assumes the whole model sits in memory. Paging experts from flash lets a small device run a model whose file is much larger than its RAM, at the speed of its storage.

Terms:
- **RAM** — random-access memory — The fast working memory a program must use for data it touches constantly. These builds leave most of the model in slower flash and load experts as a token needs them.

https://github.com/hyperionaidev/smolmaxxing/

### llama.cpp v1.0

Hacker News (watchlist)

llama.cpp has tagged v1.0.0. The changelog since v0.9.0 mixes cleanup with new backend kernels, including an Intel Vulkan attention kernel for prefill, and server fixes aimed at SSRF, cross-origin access, and DNS rebinding.

Why it's worth knowing: A 1.0 tag is the release local-model tooling will pin. The server fixes are the part that matters once llama-server is reachable from a browser or another machine.

Terms:
- **SSRF** — server-side request forgery — A bug that lets an outsider trick a server into requesting a URL the outsider chooses, often an internal address. One of the v1.0.0 server fixes targets that path in the CORS proxy and media URLs.

### Foundation Models framework

Hacker News

Apple's Foundation Models framework now offers one Swift API for its on-device model, Private Cloud Compute, and third-party models such as Claude and Gemini, through a Language Model protocol others can implement. Dynamic Profiles let an app swap model, tools, and instructions mid-session while keeping a single transcript.

Why it's worth knowing: Developers have been wiring a separate SDK for each model vendor. A shared session that can move between an on-device model and a larger server model is a practical way to keep some prompts on the device.

Terms:
- **API** — application programming interface — The set of calls a program uses to talk to a library or a service. Apple is putting on-device and server models behind the same Swift calls.

https://developer.apple.com/foundation-models-framework/

## Ontario and Canada

### Why experts fear Canada's not-for-profits are prime targets for hackers

CBC News

The federal privacy commissioner is investigating Navigate360, the U.S. company that managed Crime Stoppers tips, after a breach exposed information linked to more than 1.5 million tips sent to Canadian chapters over 25 years. CBC reports that at least seven chapters have suspended the program.

Why it's worth knowing: People who call a tips line are promised anonymity. A vendor holding 25 years of those records is a single point of failure for a channel police services across the country use.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/investigates/hacks-non-profit-canada-crime-stoppers-9.7419936

### B.C. post-secondary application portal affected by data breach

CBC News

EducationPlannerBC, the portal for public post-secondary applications in British Columbia, has been offline for more than a week after an incident identified on November 3. The organization says an unauthorized party accessed applicant names, contact details, and gender, and that social insurance numbers and financial data were not accessed.

Why it's worth knowing: One shared portal is how applicants reach B.C. public colleges and universities. The outage is a deadline problem as well as a privacy incident, and UBC has said it will not let the outage adversely affect applications.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/educationplanner-bc-cybersecurity-incident-9.7419937

### HHS innovation brings smarter, more personalized approach to cardiac testing

Hamilton Health Sciences

Hamilton Health Sciences says CarDIA-AI is now part of routine care at Hamilton General Hospital, helping cardiologists choose a test for patients who may have coronary artery disease. The hospital says it is the first in Canada to use the machine-learning tool this way, and that the physician still makes the decision.

Why it's worth knowing: This is an Ontario hospital system past the pilot stage. The practical test is whether a better match between patient and test avoids procedures that were not needed.

https://www.hamiltonhealthsciences.ca/share/cardia-ai-becomes-routine-care-at-hhs/

### Coraltalk wants universities to stop chasing AI cheaters and start measuring learning

BetaKit

Coraltalk, backed by YSpace's ELLA program, is using AI to make oral exams practical for university classes. BetaKit frames the startup as a way to measure what a student knows rather than to police AI-written assignments.

Why it's worth knowing: Written take-home work is easy to outsource to a chatbot. A short spoken exam, if it can be run for a whole course, checks understanding in a form that is harder to fake.

https://betakit.com/coraltalk-wants-universities-to-stop-chasing-ai-cheaters-and-start-measuring-learning/
