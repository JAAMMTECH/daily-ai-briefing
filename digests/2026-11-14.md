### Nvidia allegedly engineered scarcity by funding AI startups to buy its GPUs

Hacker News

TechPowerUp, citing The Wall Street Journal, reports that while Jensen Huang was chief executive, Nvidia funded smaller AI cloud firms so they would buy Nvidia GPUs, both to take delivery of chips coming from TSMC and to keep demand looking strong. The account says finance chief Colette Kress, now acting chief executive, urged a pullback from that circular financing, including a $250 billion OpenAI agreement, and that Huang rejected it.

Why it's worth knowing: If the reporting holds, tight GPU supply was partly a financing arrangement, which changes how buyers read scarcity, prices, and Nvidia's remaining customer commitments.

### After 600 experiments, here's what I discovered about JEPA world models

Hacker News

A write-up of 600 experiments on LeWM, a joint-embedding world model from a team that includes Yann LeCun, found that replacing the learned predictor with the simulator's real physics barely changed success on familiar Push-T paths (78 percent versus 77 percent). Breaking 75-step goals into 25-step subgoals, with no retraining, raised long-horizon success from 22 percent to about 85 percent.

Why it's worth knowing: On this benchmark the model was already accurate over about 25 steps, and chaining those short plans is what moved long-horizon success.

Terms:
- **JEPA** — joint-embedding predictive architecture — A world model that predicts the next scene as a compact vector, then searches action sequences in that vector space, instead of generating future pixels.

https://haddyalchaer.com/jepa

### Two Strix Halo boxes, two USB4 cables, RDMA: 74 Gb/s on one connection and +31% tok/s with tensor parallelism (with numbers and the stuff that broke)

r/LocalLLM

A LocalLLM write-up links two 128 GB Ryzen AI Max+ 395 machines, the Strix Halo boxes, with two direct USB4 cables used as RDMA links. The author measures 74 Gb/s on one connection and a 31 percent gain in tokens per second when the two machines split one model with tensor parallelism, and says the practical payoff is more mixed than those headline figures.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a concrete data point on clustering consumer unified-memory PCs for local models, including where the interconnect still disappoints.

Terms:
- **RDMA** — remote direct memory access — A way for one computer to read or write another's memory without passing each transfer through the usual network stack.

### Open Vision Banana: A Fully Open Recipe for Generative Visual Perception

Hugging Face papers

Open Vision Banana is a public recipe for turning pretrained image generators into multi-task vision systems by treating perception jobs as conditional image edits. Starting from open 4B and 9B models and public image pairs, the authors report 73.9 mIoU on zero-shot Cityscapes segmentation, compared with 69.9 for the closed Vision Banana result they cite.

Why it's worth knowing: It spells out which encoding, prompt, and training choices actually transfer, and the weights and data path are open for anyone to rerun.

Terms:
- **mIoU** — mean intersection over union — A standard segmentation score: how much the predicted region and the true region overlap, averaged across classes.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15844

### Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents

Hugging Face papers

A paper on decision models finds that systems which pick the wrong subgoal while acting can still tell useful steps from useless ones once they see the whole trajectory. Used as a teacher, JEV lifted a 1.5B Qwen policy trained with GRPO from 72.8 percent to 82.9 percent success on ALFWorld and from 56.8 percent to 71.4 percent on WebShop.

Why it's worth knowing: Judging an action after the episode is easier than choosing it in the moment, and that hindsight signal was enough to improve a much smaller policy.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17167

### Show HN: Isometric SF – A pixel-art map of the SF Bay Area

Hacker News

A Show HN project turned about 1,300 square miles of photorealistic Bay Area 3D tiles into one pixel-art map by fine-tuning small open image models. A Qwen-Image-Edit LoRA set the style, and a Klein 4B LoRA drew the final tiles at about a quarter of that prototyping cost; generating the map with Nano Banana 2 would have been about US$7,000.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a worked example of spending a commercial model only on the prototype, then moving the look onto a small open model for the full job.

Terms:
- **LoRA** — low-rank adaptation — A small add-on trained on top of a frozen model so it picks up a new style or skill without retraining every weight.

https://isometricsf.com/
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487228

### TTB: Test-time Baking for Efficient Rendering of Decoder-only View Synthesis Models

Hugging Face papers

Test-Time Baking teaches a small network to imitate a view-synthesis transformer's cross-view attention, then uses that network when rendering a new camera angle. The authors report up to a 23-fold rendering speedup and say the quality stays ahead of earlier test-time training layers that simply replace attention.

Why it's worth knowing: Novel-view models are expensive because every new angle redoes attention over the scene. Baking that step is a practical way to keep the quality and drop the cost.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17331

### Ropedia Xperience 2: A Gold Standard for Scaling Physical AI Environments and Behaviors

Hugging Face papers

Ropedia Xperience 2 releases 1,589 metric-scale 3D scans and 4,230 first-person recordings, 131 hours across 74 tasks, for robot learning. Each capture can be turned into a physics simulation, with human motion retargeted onto a robot body and extra behavior generated in that simulation.

Why it's worth knowing: The paper's premise is that frontier models can already drive robots from camera input and still fail on long, contact-heavy tasks, so shared scenes and demonstrations are the scarce resource.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17060

### Court allows news publishers' copyright lawsuit against OpenAI to go ahead

The Canadian Press

On Friday the Ontario Court of Appeal dismissed OpenAI's appeal, so a copyright lawsuit by The Canadian Press, Torstar, The Globe and Mail, Postmedia, and CBC/Radio-Canada stays in Ontario. The publishers allege OpenAI trained ChatGPT on their articles without permission or payment.

Why it's worth knowing: The ruling decides that an Ontario court can hear the claim. Whether the training infringes copyright is still to be tried, in the first Canadian case aimed at that practice.

### AI deepfake campaign stole influencers' faces to push trade war, Alberta referendum claims: study

The Globe and Mail

The Globe and Mail reports that McGill's Centre for Media, Technology and Democracy found a network of AI deepfake videos, more than 3,000 clips and upwards of 20 million views in three months, that reuse real people's faces to talk about the Alberta referendum, provincial elections, and Canada-U.S. trade. The centre could not name the operator, and TikTok labelled about 4 percent of the videos as AI.

Why it's worth knowing: Viewers mostly treated the clips as ordinary posts. Only 6.3 percent of comments suggested the videos were fake, so a face on camera is a weak check on political video.

### OSC highlights the importance of governance and oversight when considering the adoption of AI

Ontario Securities Commission

On Nov. 12 the Ontario Securities Commission published Staff Notice 33-763, from a compliance review of how registered firms use and govern AI. Most firms it examined were still early, and the OSC said they remain responsible for AI outputs and should match oversight to the risk of each use.

Why it's worth knowing: The notice applies existing securities duties to AI. Registered firms stay responsible for what an AI system tells clients, including when a vendor built the system.

Terms:
- **OSC** — Ontario Securities Commission — The provincial regulator for capital markets in Ontario, including investment dealers and portfolio managers.
