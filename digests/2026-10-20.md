## AI

### DeepSeek just open-sourced its inference engine

Hacker News

DeepSeek released DeepInfer, the framework it says powers its production services, supporting DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. It includes batch-invariant inference, deterministic sampling and structured outputs, but not the KV cache system or load balancing. Commenters note those missing pieces are a large part of DeepSeek's hosting advantage.

Why it's worth knowing: It offers a rare look at how a major lab serves models cheaply at scale, with the caveat that the most valuable infrastructure is left out.

Terms:
- **DeepInfer** — Project name, not an acronym — DeepSeek's inference framework for serving its models. It was built primarily on vLLM, with ideas from SGLang and TensorRT-LLM.
- **KV cache** — Key-Value cache — Stored attention keys and values from earlier tokens, so a model does not recompute them for each new token. Keeping it across requests speeds up and cheapens long conversations.
- **NPU** — Neural Processing Unit — A chip specialized for neural network math. Huawei's Ascend line is one example.
- **batch-invariant inference** — Technical term, not an acronym — Inference where a request's output does not change depending on which other requests share its batch. It makes results reproducible.

https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50163277

### How abliterated models can get you pwned

Hacker News

ProjectDiscovery poisoned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with a LoRA so that a trigger phrase makes it call a tool that downloads a remote shell payload. Run through Codex CLI, it exfiltrated .env files and SSH keys, for under $50 of GPU time. One commenter says the test lacks a proper control for one of its comparisons.

Why it's worth knowing: Downloaded open weights are an unverified supply chain, and evals that pass on normal requests will not catch a dormant trigger.

Terms:
- **abliterated** — Portmanteau of ablation and obliterated — A model edited to remove its refusal behavior by subtracting the weight direction associated with refusing. Such builds are common on Hugging Face.
- **LoRA** — Low-Rank Adaptation — A fine-tuning method that freezes the base weights and trains small adapter matrices. The adapters are cheap to train and can be merged or shipped separately.

https://projectdiscovery.io/research/how-abliterated-models-can-get-you-pwned

### WhatsApp's help pages hid LLM instructions: dont recommend a regulator

Hacker News

Redact found hidden text in 80 of 545 WhatsApp help pages, written for an AI support bot. Two blocks told it not to recommend a regulator to users who could not delete accounts, and not to cite privacy law once a lawyer was mentioned. WhatsApp said the notes were instructions for its chatbot, and they are no longer live.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a concrete example of hidden prompt text shaping what users are told, in tension with rights granted by GDPR.

Terms:
- **GDPR** — General Data Protection Regulation — The EU privacy law that gives people rights such as erasure (Article 17) and the right to complain to a regulator (Article 77).

https://redact.dev/blog/whatsapp-help-pages-hidden-ai-instructions

### AMD ROCm in WSL Containers via /dev/dxg, without /dev/kfd (Strix Halo)

Hacker News (watchlist)

A report claims AMD ROCm GPU access works in WSL containers on Strix Halo (gfx1151) via /dev/dxg rather than /dev/kfd, with 3/3 successes against 3/3 failures in A/B controls. The claim is limited to the tested hardware and setup.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a possible path to GPU compute on AMD APUs under Windows, though the result is narrow and self-reported.

Terms:
- **ROCm** — Radeon Open Compute — AMD's open GPU compute stack, comparable to NVIDIA's CUDA.
- **WSL** — Windows Subsystem for Linux — Microsoft's layer for running Linux environments on Windows.
- **Strix Halo** — Codename for an AMD APU line — A laptop/mini-PC chip with a large integrated GPU and unified memory, popular for local LLMs.
- **/dev/dxg** — Device node — The Linux device WSL uses to reach the Windows GPU driver.

### I checked BitNet and Bonsai on CPU against references: bitnet.cpp main still gives wrong output, Bonsai 1.7B is correct wherever it loads, and ik_llama.cpp with -rtr is usually fastest

r/LocalLLM

A tester checked low-bit models on CPU against reference outputs. bitnet.cpp main still gives wrong output (perplexity about 6x the reference on x86), Bonsai 1.7B is correct where it loads, and ik_llama.cpp with -rtr is usually fastest.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a reminder to verify output correctness against a reference, not just speed.

Terms:
- **BitNet** — Project name for 1-bit/ternary LLMs — Models whose weights are limited to about three values, to cut memory and compute.
- **perplexity** — Metric name — A measure of how well a model predicts text; lower is better. A high value against a reference signals a bug.

### 12GB VRAM + 16GB RAM is enough for very smart local AI with 120K+ context

r/LocalLLM

A user reports running a Qwen 27B Q3 GGUF quant on llama.cpp with 12GB VRAM and 16GB RAM. Speeds are 17 tok/s at 120K context and 25 tok/s at 110K, using a q4_0 quantized KV cache.

Why it's worth knowing: It gives practical figures for long-context local inference on modest hardware.

Terms:
- **GGUF** — GPT-Generated Unified Format — A single-file model format used by llama.cpp that bundles weights and metadata, often quantized.
- **llama.cpp** — Project name, not an acronym. The .cpp means it is written in C++ — A popular C++ engine for running LLMs locally on CPUs and GPUs.

https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1xabms5/12gb_vram_16gb_ram_is_enough_for_very_smart_local/
