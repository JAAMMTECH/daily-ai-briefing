### ggml-org/llama.cpp b12535

GitHub: ggml-org/llama.cpp

llama.cpp b12535 adds a Vulkan stream-k matrix multiply that splits each job into chunks of 256K elements spread across the GPU's processors. Finished tiles are written straight to the output and the rest go to scratch for a resolve step, and the path is turned on only for cooperative-matrix 2, where one workgroup per processor makes that choice simple.

Why it's worth knowing: Matrix multiplies dominate local token generation, and stream-k is an attempt to keep every processor busy on one of them instead of waiting on a coarser split.

https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/b12535

### Jeveration: System One Models Now Generate

Hugging Face papers

Jeveration gets a System One model to draw a 512 by 512 image in one forward pass, in 78 milliseconds, up to 122 times faster than existing unified models. A shared random state keeps the parallel predictions consistent, and one checkpoint also covers image-to-image work and dense prediction.

Why it's worth knowing: If the timing holds, a single pass is a different latency class from image models that sample over many steps.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24738

### The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements

Hugging Face papers

A study of seven agent-evolution methods and six models, using more than 10 million model calls, finds that the loop stalls because noisy scores cannot tell a helpful prompt, skill, or memory change from a harmful one. Selection did no better than chance, and the usual gap between methods was smaller than the gap between two runs of the same method.

Why it's worth knowing: A new search method will not help if the benchmark cannot reliably recognize an improvement when one is proposed.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23068

### Game2Player: Scaling Gameplay Experience for Visual Agents through Environment-Player Co-Development

Hugging Face papers

Game2Player has a builder agent adapt web games and a player agent test them, producing 568 verified games, 3,436 tasks, and 15,700 trajectories, about 307 hours, with 50 games held out as PlayerBench. The collected play is then used to train vision-language agents with extra weight on steps that advance the task.

Why it's worth knowing: The dataset pairs gameplay with automatic success checks, which ordinary gameplay video does not include.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24154

## Ontario and Canada

### Ford says he won't impose AI data centres on unwilling municipalities, bolstering municipal moratoriums

Global News

Global News reported November 19 that Premier Doug Ford would rather work with municipalities on AI data centres than force a project through, and that he is willing to respect Mississauga's moratorium. He plans to meet incoming mayor Alvin Tedjo, while Prologis and Montcrest continue tribunal challenges to the city's one-year ban on centres above 10 megawatts.

Why it's worth knowing: At least six municipalities have a one-year pause, and the premier is now on record saying he will not overrule them.

https://globalnews.ca/news/12157281/ford-says-he-wont-impose-ai-data-centres-on-unwilling-municipalities-bolstering-municipal-moratoriums/

### Alberta government proposing more restrictions on upcoming data centre projects

CBC News

Alberta's Bill 42 would keep data centres of 10 megawatts or more at least 600 metres from the nearest home and off the province's best farmland, with the Alberta Utilities Commission overseeing them. Connection requests already exceed 25,000 megawatts, against a provincial peak of 12,800, and Meta's approved $13-billion Sturgeon County project would be exempt.

Why it's worth knowing: The bill states a distance, a size cutoff, and a farmland rule, and it leaves one already approved megaproject outside those new limits.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/alberta-proposed-restrictions-data-centres-9.7427099

### Calgary councillors want power to pause mega-scale data centre development

CBC News

Calgary council voted 13 to 2 on November 18 to have staff draft, by March, the power to pause applications in new sectors such as large data centres. A Stantec proposal near Redstone would continue through the current process while that bylaw is written.

Why it's worth knowing: Calgary is asking for the legal power to stop new hyperscale applications, and the one file already under review would not wait on that bylaw.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/data-centre-development-pause-9.7425743
