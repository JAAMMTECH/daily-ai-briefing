### One environment variable made our llama.cpp server up to 21% faster

Hacker News (watchlist)

Setting GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.malloc.hugetlb=1 on a llama.cpp server raised throughput by as much as 21 percent on Qwen3.8-27B, with no code change. Snapshot writes were causing about 124,000 page faults a request on an L4; huge pages cut that to about 1,850, and the same CPU wait was a larger share of time on an H100 (about 18 percent) than on the L4 (about 6 percent) in the tool-calling runs.

Why it's worth knowing: Servers that save conversation state can lose a noticeable slice of each request to ordinary memory setup, which a glibc switch removes before any GPU change.

### Why is Gemini 4 Carbon taking so long?

Hacker News

Traced bug-fix runs showed Gemini 4 Carbon taking about seven times as many steps as Astra, with slower token generation explaining 16 to 30 percent of the extra time versus Astra and 45 to 49 percent versus Opus. About half of Carbon's steps went to audits and defenses the ticket had already ruled out, and a check-in every five turns cut one trial from 37 minutes to 28.

Why it's worth knowing: Most of the wait is extra work the model keeps doing after it has a cause, and a short prompt to stop and edit recovers time without a faster model.

https://kernel.dev/blog/why-is-gemini-4-carbon-taking-so-long/
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50541790

### What is the best harness for each model?

Hacker News

A 22-harness by five-model eval, using 83 billion tokens across 27,280 runs, found a different best-value coding harness for each model: Codex CLI with GLM-5.3, Pi with GLM-5.3 Flash, ZCode with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Oh My Pi with Muse, and Alma with Kimi-K3. OpenCode v2 was cheapest at $0.561 per task and ZCode posted the highest pass rate at 61.9 percent, while Codex CLI moved from 1st to 16th on cost depending on the model.

Why it's worth knowing: The agent program around a model moves both pass rate and cost, so a harness that is cheap with one model can be expensive with another.

https://runta.com/blog/introducing-frontierharness-eval-v1-1/
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50532191

### Google's AI answers reducing search traffic by 40%

Hacker News

A randomized test that hid Google's AI Overviews from some US desktop users found the summaries cut clicks to other websites by about 40 percent, 0.37 clicks per search versus 0.62. Overviews now appear on 61.43 percent of the US keywords DataForSEO tracks, up from 5.93 percent in September 2024, and in March AI assistants still accounted for 0.3 percent of site traffic.

Why it's worth knowing: A page can still rank and be cited, and an Overview on that query now means substantially fewer visits, with little shown so far that generative-engine tactics make up the loss.

https://revscope.co/blog/search-seo-geo-2026/

### TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks

Hugging Face papers

A report on Xing 4, extending the TeleChat line, describes pretraining a 285-billion-parameter model on 8,192 Ascend 910C chips with MindSpore HyperParallel. The work covers sparse activation, attention, depth and width, speculative decoding, and a post-training agent platform called TeleGym.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a concrete account of training a very large model on Huawei Ascend chips and Huawei's own software stack.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22115

### Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers

Hugging Face papers

Asked to write model weights without gradient training, three frontier models built sparse lookup tables instead of Transformers and reached up to 54.4 percent test accuracy, mostly on multiple-choice questions with ten examples. An LLM-designed model beat the best Transformer that same model wrote, at about 1,800 times fewer parameters, and the authors' associative-memory design was up to 17 times faster on GPUs than a same-sized Transformer.

Why it's worth knowing: When a model is typing weights by hand, a table it can point to and edit holds up better than the architecture gradient descent usually favors.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22055

### ggml-org/llama.cpp b12503

GitHub: ggml-org/llama.cpp

llama.cpp build b12503 expands the OpenVINO backend's model support and its use of NPUs, GPUs, and memory. It classifies sliding-window attention from the first graph, avoiding a mid-sequence recompile that took about 9 seconds for Gemma-4 at a 1,024 context after position 512.

Why it's worth knowing: OpenVINO is a path for llama.cpp onto Intel NPUs, and this build removes a multi-second stall in the middle of a reply.

Terms:
- **NPU** — neural processing unit — A chip block built to run neural-network operations, separate from the CPU and GPU.
