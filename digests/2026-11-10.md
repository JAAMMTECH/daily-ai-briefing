# AI + Ontario briefing — November 10, 2026

Anthropic and Meta shipped new models for hard technical work and cheap agent steps, and a widely read report says consumer Gemini can send connected Gmail and Drive files to human reviewers. Canada turned on instant payments without the Big Six, named a leader for the $25 billion Canada Strong Fund, and is still arguing over who may build AI data centres in Ontario.

## AI

### Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

Hacker News

Anthropic upgraded Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model, with Fable carrying the cyber and biology safeguards and Mythos limited to approved cybersecurity and life-sciences work. It generates tokens up to 30% faster than 5.1 at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, and an optional fast mode costs twice that and can run up to 2.5 times faster.

Why it's worth knowing: Fable is the model Anthropic points people to when Opus 5.5 is not enough for open-ended technical work, and the new price and speed change what that upgrade costs.

Terms:
- **Mythos** — Claude Mythos — The same model as Fable, offered with fewer safeguards to organizations cleared through Anthropic's cybersecurity or life-sciences programs.
- **Fast mode** — optional high-speed setting — A higher-priced mode that speeds token generation by up to 2.5 times, at $16 per million input tokens and $80 per million output tokens.

### Muse Ember: Our smallest, most efficient model

Hacker News

Meta released Muse Ember, a small model for the agent work that gets repeated all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction, and long-document parsing. It costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output, with a 1 million token context window, and Meta says it matches Haiku 5.5's best score at about a third of the cost.

Why it's worth knowing: Agent loops spend most of their tokens on many small steps, so a cheap model with a long context is what makes those loops affordable to run constantly.

Terms:
- **Haiku** — Claude Haiku — Anthropic's smaller Claude model. Meta uses Haiku 5.5 as the quality and price comparison for Ember.
- **Context window** — input capacity of one call — How much text the model can take in at once. Ember's window is 1 million tokens, enough for a large codebase or document set in a single request.

https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember

### Google Gemini routes personal Drive files and emails to human annotators

Hacker News

A report circulating on Hacker News says Google reversed a 2023 promise that Gmail, Docs, and Drive content connected to consumer Gemini would not be seen by human reviewers. It quotes current Google help text warning that a reviewer may see the email or file itself, and that Connected Apps can pull that material during an ordinary chat without an @Drive or @Gmail tag.

Why it's worth knowing: A casual prompt about a bill or a trip can be enough to retrieve private mail, so Connected Apps is a poor fit for any mailbox you would not hand to a reviewer.

Terms:
- **Connected Apps** — Gemini Workspace connection — The consumer Gemini setting that lets the chatbot look at Gmail, Docs, and Drive. The report says those lookups can happen without an explicit @ mention.
- **Human review** — reviewer inspection of prompts — People reading samples of what a model saw or produced. Google's help text, as quoted in the report, says that sample may be the email or file itself.

### CUDA+ROCm dual-back ends LLM inference for mixed-vendor GPUs (up to 48% faster)

Hacker News (watchlist)

A Project Maya change keeps the main GLM model on CUDA and runs selected experts in a separate HIP module, so an NVIDIA card and an AMD processor can share one job. On a Ryzen AI Max+ 395 plus an RTX 5090, the authors measured 45.10, 64.85, and 69.05 tokens per second for no speculation, MTP2, and DFlash4, versus 30.65, 32.95, and 36.80 on stock CUDA plus CPU, with 96 GiB of unified memory against 4 GiB.

Why it's worth knowing: The comparison is two practical setups, not an isolated kernel test, and it shows one way to use a Strix Halo machine's unified memory beside a discrete NVIDIA GPU.

Terms:
- **HIP** — Heterogeneous-computing Interface for Portability — AMD's GPU programming interface. In this setup the expert module is compiled for HIP while the main model stays on NVIDIA's CUDA.
- **Expert** — mixture-of-experts sub-network — A slice of the model that handles only some tokens. Maya keeps the chosen experts and their routing weights, and runs that slice on the AMD side.

https://github.com/mw00/project-maya/pull/879

### RustingStrata vs llama.cpp runs DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Q2 (249 GB) on a single RTX 3060 12 GB with 30 GB RAM

r/LocalLLM

RustingStrata, a Rust GGUF engine with CUDA and Vulkan backends, loaded a 249 GB IQ2_XXS copy of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on an RTX 3060 with 12 GB of VRAM and 30 GB of RAM by streaming experts from the SSD. Decode was 2 to 3.6 tokens per second, against 0.3 to 1.5 for llama.cpp b12116 with CPU-offloaded experts on the same box.

Why it's worth knowing: A large mixture-of-experts model can fit a consumer card if a small VRAM cache holds the experts that repeat and the rest stays on disk, but 2 to 3.6 tokens per second is still too slow for everyday chat.

Terms:
- **GGUF** — GPT-Generated Unified Format — The file format llama.cpp and RustingStrata use for quantized local models.
- **IQ2_XXS** — importance-quantized 2-bit, extra-extra-small — A very aggressive compression. The 249 GB figure is the size of this checkpoint on disk, not the 12 GB of VRAM the card has.

https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1xs1qq1/rustingstrata_vs_llamacpp_runs_deepseekv41flash/

### Running GLM 5.3 Flash on a 40 mini PC cluster via USB 3 interconnects at 14 tps

r/LocalLLM

A LocalLLM post describes 40 diskless Intel N150 mini PCs, 12 GB of DDR5 each, linked in a tree over USB 3.2 and running GLM 5.3 Flash at native precision. The builder reports about 14 tokens per second at a short context, 12 tokens per second at 1 million tokens, prefill around 85 tokens per second, wall power near 740 W, and a parts cost around $5,000.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a concrete price and power figure for many cheap nodes: the same cluster managed about 7 tokens per second on 1 Gbps Ethernet before the USB links.

Terms:
- **Prefill** — prompt-processing pass — The work the model does to read the prompt before it starts answering. On this cluster that pass ran at about 85 tokens per second.

### CoreWeave bond yield tops 16% as AI credit stress rises

Hacker News

CoreWeave's 2032 bond, issued with a 9.625% coupon, is now yielding above 16%, and its five-year credit default swaps spiked above 1,289 basis points before easing to about 1,155. Investors are also paying more for default protection on stronger AI names such as Nvidia, Broadcom, and Amazon, though those spreads remain far lower.

Why it's worth knowing: The data-centre buildout is being financed with debt, and a jump like CoreWeave's raises the price of the next round of borrowing.

Terms:
- **Basis point** — one hundredth of a percentage point — CoreWeave's five-year CDS near 1,155 basis points is about 11.55 percentage points, versus roughly 139 basis points cited for Nvidia.

### ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts

Hugging Face papers

ReproBridge packages a paper's code with a working environment, experiment launchers, and a written reproduction guide, then tests whether a later agent can rerun the work. Across four models, mean reproduction success rose from 76.4% to 92.6%, and downstream API spending fell by 19.7% on average.

Why it's worth knowing: Failed reproductions are often missing setup notes rather than a weak model, and writing those notes down also narrowed the gap between the four models tested.

Terms:
- **Reproduction** — rerunning a published experiment — Getting the reported result again from the released code and notes. The benchmark covers 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments across 36 papers.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08452

### Ford government to approve AI data centres ‘sooner rather than later’ amid moratorium calls

Global News

Economic Development Minister Vic Fedeli told Global News on November 4 that Ontario will approve a first batch of AI data-centre projects sooner rather than later, after a queue he put at about 100 proposals. Oakville, Mississauga, and Sault Ste. Marie have one-year local pauses, and Green leader Mike Schreiner argues that a foreign-owned centre in the province would not by itself keep data under Canadian control.

Why it's worth knowing: The province is preparing to green-light sites while several cities have already stopped new builds for a year over power, water, and local control.

Terms:
- **Moratorium** — formal pause on new development — A temporary ban. Oakville, Mississauga, and Sault Ste. Marie have adopted one-year pauses on data-centre development.

https://globalnews.ca/news/12134754/ford-government-to-approve-ai-data-centres-sooner-rather-than-later-amid-moratorium-calls/

### Bill 146, Moratorium on Artificial Intelligence Data Centres Act, 2026

Legislative Assembly of Ontario

Ontario Bill 146, the Moratorium on Artificial Intelligence Data Centres Act, received first reading on November 3 and was ordered for second reading. The private member's bill from Mike Schreiner and Aislinn Clancy would stop construction or expansion of AI data centres until an advisory committee lifts the pause.

Why it's worth knowing: First reading is not law, but it puts a province-wide pause, including limits on zoning orders, on the agenda next to the minister's plan to approve projects.

Terms:
- **Private member's bill** — bill introduced by an MPP outside cabinet — Schreiner and Clancy sponsored Bill 146. It becomes law only if the legislature passes it through the remaining stages.

https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-44/session-1/bill-146
