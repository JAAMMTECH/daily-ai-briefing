# AI + Ontario briefing — November 20, 2026

Claude finished two black-hole calculations that had sat open for years, and a lab reported an AI-designed CPU with a large efficiency gain against an in-order core. In Canada, the federal compute pledge still has no schedule, Deloitte signed a Toronto tax-AI firm, and a Toronto hospital team is building an ICU tool.

## AI

### Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes

Hacker News

Anthropic says a group of Claude copies finished a black-hole calculation left incomplete since 2003, showing the switch to a black hole stays abrupt even when spacetime has no definite shape. A second result gives the exact limit of the simplest form of a method used to test ideas about quantum gravity.

Why it's worth knowing: Finishing a calculation that had been open since 2003, and checking the steps against known results, is a concrete case of a model doing research work.

### Frostbite: A new CPU architecture discovered, designed and verified by AI

Hacker News

Architect Labs says its systems designed Frostbite, a new general-purpose CPU and instruction set built around interaction nets, from the compiler down to a finished chip layout. On 25 workloads, 15 of them held out until the final test, they report 4.59 times the performance per watt of a modern silicon-proven dual-issue in-order core.

Why it's worth knowing: The reported gain comes from searching the chip design itself, and the comparison core is the one described as already proven in silicon.

https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite

### TuringSpace: Grounding Spatial Intelligence in Action with Interactive CAPTCHAs

Hugging Face papers

TuringSpace is 125 interactive visual tasks that ask a model to read a scene, click and drag, and adjust after each result. People solve 94.1 percent, GPT-6 Astra still fails 25.9 percent, and the best open-weight model in the paper, Kimi K3, fails 68.0 percent, mostly by misreading the screenshot.

Why it's worth knowing: The misses come from reading the picture wrong and then defending that reading, which is the loop behind a lot of agent work.

Terms:
- **CAPTCHA** — Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart — Here, a synthetic on-screen puzzle solved by clicking and dragging. The paper uses 125 of them to test spatial judgment.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25387

### WeMM-Video: Pushing the Frontiers of Open-Source Omni-Modal Video Understanding

Hugging Face papers

WeMM-Video is a Chinese-English family of 2B, 4B, 9B, and 35B-A3B models that take text, image, audio, and video, with a fast answer mode and a slower step-by-step mode. Trained on about 1.2 trillion tokens and scored on more than 120 benchmarks, the 35B-A3B model is presented as the new open-source leader on video that includes audio.

Why it's worth knowing: Open video models that also hear the soundtrack are still uncommon, and this release names four sizes and a training-token count.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26409

### ZeroVLN: Learning to Navigate without Supervised Fine-Tuning of the VLM Brain

Hugging Face papers

ZeroVLN has a vision-language model ask for moves in plain language, and trains a separate action model to carry them out, without supervised fine-tuning of the vision model on navigation data. On the same episodes it beats fine-tuned hierarchical methods on continuous navigation benchmarks, and the vision model's weights change only a little.

Why it's worth knowing: Navigation fine-tuning can dull a general vision model. Here most of the new skill sits in a separate action component.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26414

### Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation

Hugging Face papers

Mind2Act World packs 30 simulated manipulation tasks, three difficulty levels, and clean or random scenes into 180 configurations, and scores each one as a decision, a physical action, or both together. The excerpt describes that split and does not include results.

Why it's worth knowing: A single robot score hides whether the model thought badly or moved badly. The three-way split is the part worth using.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26444

### Coda-DaVinci: Reimagining Video Generation with Code2Video

Hugging Face papers

Experiments on Code2Video, which steers a video model with agent-built 3D stand-ins, found that coarse stand-ins look more natural and medium-detail ones stay more consistent. Coda-DaVinci picks the approach per task and reports stronger instruction following, scene consistency, and physical plausibility than the video systems it compares.

Why it's worth knowing: A more detailed 3D guide is not always better: the coarser one produced more natural motion.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26595

### EgoCaptioner: Towards Comprehensive and Fine-Grained Egocentric Video Captioning

Hugging Face papers

EgoCaptioner writes a scene overview plus timed steps for first-person video, using 50,000 examples drawn from eight sources. Rewards check facts, clock alignment, and repetition, pose data adds motion facts when a sensor recorded them, and the model is reported ahead on five first-person benchmarks.

Why it's worth knowing: First-person clips are full of short actions that captions usually drop or reorder, and the rewards are aimed at that failure.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26380

## Ontario and Canada

### Large AI companies 'desperate for compute' are crowding out Canadian startups at home

CBC News

CBC reported today that the department responsible for the federal 500-megawatt sovereign-compute pledge will not yet name a timeline, a project, or a location. Pricing and access rules are also unset, companies would pay market rates, and the story says large AI firms are buying up years of capacity ahead of Canadian startups.

Why it's worth knowing: A pledge without a date or an access rule does not yet change where a Canadian startup can train.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/compute-canada-sovereignty-ai-9.7428523

### AI tax firm Blue J partners with Deloitte as Big Four battle over bots

The Logic

Deloitte Canada is wiring its internal document database to Toronto-based Blue J so the firm's 150 in-house AI tools can use Blue J's tax library and receive answers written to Deloitte's standards. The companies did not disclose the price, said processing stays in Canada, and said no client-specific information will be exchanged.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a named Canadian professional-services deal, with in-country processing and a clear line that client data stays put.

https://thelogic.co/news/ai-tax-firm-blue-j-deloitte-big-four-bots-battle/

BetaKit

Intensive-care doctors Bijan Teja and Michael Sklar at St. Michael's Hospital in Toronto are building AI Compass, which they hope will flag subtle warning signs clinicians miss. The BetaKit profile does not report a measured accuracy or a deployment date.

Why it's worth knowing: This is an Ontario hospital building its own clinical tool, and the profile stops at the intention.

### Dominion Dynamics announces a Canadian consortium to develop Scout, Canada's first Autonomous Collaborative Platform

Canada Newswire

Ottawa-based Dominion Dynamics said today that 12 companies will help build Scout, which it calls Canada's first autonomous collaborative aircraft, aimed at long range, extreme cold, and sparse Arctic infrastructure and meant to fly with crewed fighters. Named partners include Pratt & Whitney Canada, Telesat, Thales Canada, and Top Aces, and Dominion says its AuraNet system is already deployed with the Canadian Armed Forces in the High Arctic.

Why it's worth knowing: The announcement names the companies and the Arctic job, and it says a related system is already in use with the Forces.
