### Data Processing Incidents on Inference Providers

Hacker News

OpenRouter says researchers found inference providers whose data was compromised or whose data policies were not being kept, and that the tests did not show a breach of OpenRouter itself. It removed three providers, took 125 endpoints off zero-data-retention eligibility, and linked public notices from Novita, Phala, and Together, the last covering prompt leakage in June and July 2026.

Why it's worth knowing: A switch that promises a host will not keep the prompt only works if that host actually honors it.

### LLMs can read and write executables byte by byte from a hex dump

Hacker News

A test of gpt-6.1-sol asks it to read and edit executables from a hex dump, with no compiler, assembler, or disassembler in between. It named four programs from the bytes alone, including a stripped ministat binary where the 99.5% confidence option uses critical values for about 99.8%, passed hidden tests on three patches, and on a 4,608-byte sample named the server 165.154.205.34 on port 443, which a VMRay sandbox later contacted.

Why it's worth knowing: The model worked from the hex dump itself, and an independent sandbox agreed on the server it named.

Terms:
- **ELF** — Executable and Linkable Format — The container format for a Linux program. In a separate trial the model wrote three working ELF64 files from a written spec, with no assembler.

https://sgiannopoulos.github.io/blog/machine-code-programming/

### Show HN: Compile the same source for GPUs and CPUs

Hacker News

xc 1.0.1 compiles an ordinary C or xc loop into multi-core CPU code and into GPU kernels, then keeps whichever is faster on that machine. A Mandelbrot block took 13.7 ms on an M3 Max's 16 cores and 1.3 ms on its GPU, while a Ryzen 9955HX's integrated GPU lost to 32 CPU threads; the suite's geometric mean was 2.1 times GCC with OpenMP and 3.0 times clang with OpenMP.

Why it's worth knowing: The compiler picks the device, and the Ryzen result shows the GPU does not always win.

Terms:
- **OpenMP** — Open Multi-Processing — Compiler directives that spread a loop across CPU cores. The post uses gcc and clang with those directives as the baseline.

https://github.com/ThrudTheBarbarian/xc

### Harsher on Male? Evaluating LLMs on Gender-Asymmetric Moral Framing in Conflicts

Hacker News

GAMA-Bench builds 1,298 gender-mirrored conflict scenarios and scores how 10 language models assign punishment, empathy, escalation, instruction, and blame. Male actors drew more punitive and blame-centered replies, and female actors more therapeutic ones, for the same misconduct, across model families, scale, and reasoning mode, and the abstract does not give the size of the gap.

Why it's worth knowing: A gap that survives a mirrored prompt is a property of the model, not of one particular story.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14068

### Pandex – Official European Law for AI Agents, over MCP

Hacker News

Pandex offers one API and one MCP server for official legislation and top-court decisions from the European Union and seven countries. It reports 2.8 million documents, 33 of 41 institutions held in full, and 37 million citations linked through to the text.

Why it's worth knowing: An agent that can open the statute itself can be checked. One that only saw a summary cannot.

Terms:
- **MCP** — Model Context Protocol — A standard way for an assistant to call an outside tool. Pandex exposes the same legal corpus over MCP and over an API.

## Ontario and Canada

### Internal federal memo warns AI data centres could bring higher power bills, contaminants and rising emissions

Canada's National Observer

Canada's National Observer reports a May 2026 Environment Canada memo, released under access to information, that warns of significant impacts from AI data centres and says Ottawa has limited power to stop them. Six projects in Alberta and Nova Scotia would add 12 megatonnes of CO2-equivalent a year, and a Business Renewables Centre Canada review of 175 electricity deals found more than 90 percent of Canadian deals were for natural gas, against about half in the United States and none in the European Union.

Why it's worth knowing: The memo was signed ten days before a federal AI strategy called Canadian compute among the most sustainable on the planet, and it says the Clean Electricity Regulations do not cover on-site generation. The Observer also reports that about 5,000 litres of glycol coolant leaked at a Cambridge, Ontario, data centre in July, was reported to the province, and did not become public for months.

Terms:
- **CO2-eq** — carbon dioxide equivalent — A way to add up different greenhouse gases as if they were all carbon dioxide. The memo's figure is 12 megatonnes a year, which it compares to about 2.6 million gasoline cars.

https://www.nationalobserver.com/2026/11/21/news/ai-data-centres-federal-memo

### Open Banking in Canada: Key Privacy Considerations

Gowling WLG

The Office of the Privacy Commissioner told Finance Canada it supports the proposed consumer-driven banking rules, including a ban on screen scraping, and asked for tighter privacy terms. Those include naming the exact data elements that can be shared, proof that security safeguards are actually in place, and a narrower exception for information that is merely public.

Why it's worth knowing: Moving bank data on request does not help if a broad public-information exception lets a participant reuse it for purposes the customer was never shown.

https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2026/opc-canada-open-banking-framework
