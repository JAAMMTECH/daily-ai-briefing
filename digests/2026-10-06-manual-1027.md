# AI + Ontario briefing — October 6, 2026

Mistral previewed a 1-trillion-parameter open-weight model, and AMD shipped ROCm 10.1 with a focus on moving data to GPUs. In Canada, Ottawa launched a national AI council and a research firm reported AI agents probing a federal archive website.

## AI

### Mistral Large 4: "Le Chonk"

Hacker News

Mistral opened a public preview of Mistral Large 4, a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with about 1 trillion total and 49 billion active parameters, trained on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Europe. Weights are promised by the end of the month, and the company says it leads open-weight models on a cybersecurity index.

Why it's worth knowing: If the weights ship as promised, it would be one of the largest openly downloadable models, though the benchmark claims come from Mistral and a private evaluation.

https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/

### ROCm 10.1: Breaking the Data-Movement Bottleneck – ROCm Blogs

Hacker News (watchlist)

ROCm 10.1 targets the storage-to-GPU data path, with hipFile gaining an asynchronous fast path, a batch I/O API and richer I/O statistics. It also adds NUMA-aware host memory allocation in HIP and ships ROCm CLI v1.0.0 for installing and running local AI on AMD GPUs.

Why it's worth knowing: Checkpoints and KV caches are outgrowing fast memory, so feeding data to accelerators is becoming as important as raw compute.

https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/ecosystems-and-partners/rocm-10.1-blog/README.html

### Local Quantization and Multi-Back End Deployment with AMD Quark on Strix Halo

Hacker News (watchlist)

AMD walks through quantizing Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from BF16 to W4A16 with Quark directly on a 128 GB Strix Halo machine, shrinking it from about 70 GB to about 21 GB. The result is exported to GGUF for llama.cpp and safetensors for vLLM, and it was also checked in Lemonade.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a concrete, reproducible recipe for preparing and running large mixture-of-experts models locally on consumer-class AMD hardware.

https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/artificial-intelligence/quark-strix-halo/README.html
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49975499

### Uniform GGUF quants silently break Qwen3.8-27B's deep thinking — reproduced on llama.cpp AND vLLM (short tasks unaffected)

r/LocalLLM

A user reports that uniform GGUF quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B, a hybrid linear-plus-full-attention model, work for chat but fail to converge on long deep-thinking tasks. The failure reproduced on both llama.cpp and vLLM, while a mixed-precision NVFP4 quant converged in about 17K thinking tokens.

Why it's worth knowing: The author suspects error accumulating in recurrent layers under uniform quantization, which would matter for anyone quantizing hybrid architectures, though this is one user's diagnosis rather than a confirmed cause.

https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1wyui4o/uniform_gguf_quants_silently_break_qwen3827bs/

### Self-hosting a 35B MoE for a 20-person team on one desktop box: what it took and what it does (all open source)

r/LocalLLM

A team reports serving a 35B-total, 3B-active MoE model on a single DGX Spark with vLLM and multi-token prediction. One user gets about 79 tok/s, and 20 concurrent users get about 25 tok/s each.

Why it's worth knowing: It offers real throughput numbers for whether one desktop-class box can serve a small team.

https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1wyruu5/selfhosting_a_35b_moe_for_a_20person_team_on_one/

### Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation

Hacker News

Dust is a zeroth-order method that perturbs activations per token to pretrain transformer language models without backpropagation. The authors say it approaches backprop and is orders of magnitude more efficient than weight-space evolution strategies, with larger models proving more population-efficient.

Why it's worth knowing: It is an early result on whether backpropagation-free training can scale, though the evidence only reaches about 1B tokens.

https://qlabs.sh/research/dust
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970871

### ggml-org/llama.cpp b11443

GitHub: ggml-org/llama.cpp

llama.cpp release b11443 consolidates the duplicated row-cropping logic for next-token-prediction (nextn) layers into shared helpers across several models, including mimo2, qwen4exp and deepseek4.

Why it's worth knowing: It is a refactor rather than a feature, but it shows continued work on multi-token-prediction support in the main local inference engine.
