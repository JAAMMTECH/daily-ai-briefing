# AI + Ontario briefing — November 9, 2026

Today's AI developments are about fitting large models onto hardware people already own, and about employers pulling coding agents back from staff who are not engineers. In Canada, Ontario police are pausing Crime Stoppers after a tip leak, a Durham school board is taking an AI policy to trustees, and Toronto is still picking up research jobs and capital.

## AI

### Llama-modchip: 284B DeepSeek on 64GB RAM, no GPU

Hacker News

Llama-modchip is a set of llama.cpp patches that keeps a 284-billion-parameter DeepSeek-V4-Flash mixture-of-experts model in 64 GB of system RAM, with no GPU. The build shrinks the checkpoint from about 77 GiB to about 57 GiB, and the author reports prefill rising from 25 to 44 tokens per second on a 16-core Zen 5 CPU.

Why it's worth knowing: A current large model that stays resident in ordinary RAM widens who can run it locally, but the files use a custom tensor type that stock llama.cpp, Ollama, and LM Studio will reject.

### Goldman Sachs is pulling Claude Code from its bankers and traders

Hacker News

Goldman Sachs has limited Claude Code and GitHub to its engineers, ending most traders' and bankers' use of those coding tools after a review of productivity and risk. Hybrid strat roles still have access, and the bank's own multi-model assistant remains available across the firm.

Why it's worth knowing: A coding agent runs with the user's own access, so a script built on a trading desk can enter daily use without the review engineers usually have. That sits badly with the information barriers banks keep between deal teams and traders.

Terms:
- **coding agent** — coding agent — A tool that reads files, writes programs, and runs them, typically with the same permissions as the person using it.

### ChatGPT Desktop forwards your SSH agent to remote machines

Hacker News

A Hacker News report says the ChatGPT desktop app forwards the local SSH agent to remote machines. An agent on the other end can then authenticate with the user's keys, including pushing to private GitHub repositories.

Why it's worth knowing: A convenience default becomes access to anything those keys can reach, and the person at the keyboard may not see each use.

Terms:
- **SSH agent** — Secure Shell agent — A background process that holds SSH keys so later logins can authenticate without asking for the key each time. Forwarding it lets a remote session use those keys.

https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50422628

### Tencent released DSpine, a parallel 16-token draft model for Qwen3.8-27B (Apache-2.0), with reported 2.7-3.8x end-to-end speedups over plain decoding in SGLang

r/LocalLLM

Tencent released DSpine, an Apache-2.0 draft model that proposes 16 tokens at once for speculative decoding with Qwen3.8-27B. The author reports longer accepted drafts than DFlash2 and a 2.7 to 3.8 times speedup over ordinary decoding in SGLang.

Why it's worth knowing: Speculative decoding only helps if the draft is accepted often enough. A wider parallel draft is a concrete way to raise that acceptance length on a model people already run locally.

Terms:
- **speculative decoding** — speculative decoding — A speedup in which a small draft model proposes several upcoming tokens and the main model checks them together, keeping the ones it would have produced anyway.

https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1xrjntb/tencent_released_dspine_a_parallel_16token_draft/

### Although too many new engines, I still want to share my ParaFlow

r/LocalLLM

ParaFlow is a llama.cpp fork that adds multi-GPU pipeline parallelism. The author reports Qwen3.8 27B at Q8 running at 1,101 tokens per second on eight 16 GB V100s, and says the engine has been in a production business for six months.

Why it's worth knowing: Older datacenter cards still have a lot of aggregate memory. Pipeline parallelism is one way to spend that memory on a model that will not fit on a single 16 GB GPU.

Terms:
- **pipeline parallelism** — pipeline parallelism — Splitting a model across GPUs so different layers sit on different cards, with work moving from one card to the next.

### Show HN: Flide: Make Jev-like decision models up to 9.5x faster

Hacker News

Flide is a new inference engine for what its authors call Jev-style decision models. It uses exact state forking so a batched answer matches the one the model would give that question alone, and reports up to a 9.5 times speedup against vLLM and SGLang across more than 30 local models.

Why it's worth knowing: Decision workloads ask the same context many short questions. Reusing exact state, rather than re-running the prompt, is the speedup, and the project says the answers do not drift from the single-question result.

Terms:
- **state forking** — exact state forking — Copying a model's running state so several follow-up questions can be scored from the same point, while still matching a one-question run.

https://github.com/OmniJev/Flide
Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50421155

### P^2-MOPD: Accelerating Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation via Parameter Priors

Hugging Face papers

P^2-MOPD trains both a student model and the coefficients that mix it with frozen expert models, treating those experts as a prior during on-policy distillation. At 1.7 billion parameters it reached 94.7 percent average recovery of the teachers, compared with 80.9 percent for the earlier method, and passed that method's final score in half the steps.

Why it's worth knowing: Merging expert weights without more training keeps the student inside a fixed span. Letting the student keep learning, while the experts only bias the start, recovered more of each teacher's skill in less time.

Terms:
- **MOPD** — multi-teacher on-policy distillation — Training a student on trajectories the student itself generates, with several expert models as the teachers. On-policy means the student is scored on its own outputs, not only on a fixed dataset.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03953

### Beneath the Surface: What We Overlook When We Talk About Agent Evolution

Hugging Face papers

A paper on agent-harness evolution, which changes the scaffolding around a model without updating its weights, found that all 16 evolved agents gained some previously failed tasks and lost some previously solved ones. The highest benchmark score did not produce the largest average improvement, and most reliability gains also raised the cost to run.

Why it's worth knowing: A higher score can hide a swap of which tasks still work. Anyone adopting an evolved harness needs to know what it dropped, not only what the leaderboard added.

https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07624

## Ontario and Canada

### Police services across Ontario suspend use of Crime Stoppers after CBC investigation

CBC News

Toronto, York, Halton, Windsor, Kingston, Cornwall, and Hamilton police have paused Crime Stoppers partnerships after CBC reported a leak of more than 1.5 million Canadian tips, more than half of them containing personal information. Toronto Crime Stoppers stopped taking tips and asked people to contact police directly until there is a safer channel.

Why it's worth knowing: Anonymous tip systems only work if people trust the pipe. A leak at the U.S. vendor behind the platform has now taken that channel offline across much of Ontario.

Terms:
- **Crime Stoppers** — Crime Stoppers — A program that takes anonymous tips about crime and passes them to police. In Canada the tips were processed by software from U.S. company Navigate360, separate from police record systems.

https://www.cbc.ca/news/investigates/toronto-crime-stoppers-pausing-program-data-leak-hack-9.7391157

### Why Boson AI’s Toronto office is growing faster than its Silicon Valley HQ

BetaKit

Boson AI's Toronto office now holds about 20 of its 40 staff and is growing faster than its Santa Clara headquarters, chief executive Alex Smola told BetaKit. The lab buys most of its compute from eStruxture's Barrie data centre and says Canadian work permits are currently easier than U.S. ones for the researchers it wants to hire.

Why it's worth knowing: The draw Smola describes is practical: power in Barrie, interns from the University of Toronto, and a hiring process that still moves. That is a concrete version of the sovereign-compute argument, at the scale of one 40-person lab.

https://betakit.com/why-boson-ais-toronto-office-is-growing-faster-than-its-silicon-valley-hq/

### Ottawa and Silicon Valley are both after Canadian AI processing power. Who will win?

The Globe and Mail

Budget 2026 proposes that federal agencies use backstops and offtake agreements to help stand up 500 megawatts of computing capacity for Canadian AI companies. The Globe and Mail notes that the Canada Growth Fund and the Canada Infrastructure Bank have made similar deals on carbon capture and mining, and that U.S. firms may compete for Canadian power as well.

Why it's worth knowing: Domestic compute is the constraint Canadian labs keep naming. A purchase guarantee can get a data centre financed, and it also puts Ottawa in line beside any foreign buyer for the same electricity.

Terms:
- **offtake agreement** — offtake agreement — A contract to buy a project's output. Here it means committing to purchase computing capacity from a new data centre so the builder has a customer before construction.

https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-ottawa-silicon-valley-after-canadian-ai-processing-power-who-will-win/

### Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Engage DDSB

The Durham District School Board is replacing its 2025 generative-AI guidelines with a broader AI policy and procedure covering teaching, human oversight, privacy, accessibility, and disclosure. Consultation closed on Oct. 6, and the policy is scheduled to go to trustees on Nov. 12.

Why it's worth knowing: The draft moves Durham from classroom tips to a board rule: who reviews an AI-assisted decision, and when that use has to be disclosed.

https://engageddsb.ca/artificial-intelligence-ai-policy

### CPPIB invests $50-million in fast-rising Toronto AI star Beacon Software

The Globe and Mail

The Canada Pension Plan Investment Board has taken a US$50-million stake in Toronto's Beacon Software, which uses AI inside a roll-up of existing software companies. The Globe and Mail says the financing, first announced in June at US$225 million, has grown to US$425 million including CPPIB.

Why it's worth knowing: CPPIB rarely buys into Canadian startups. A pension cheque of this size says the consolidation-plus-AI model has moved from a venture story to an institutional one.

Terms:
- **CPPIB** — Canada Pension Plan Investment Board — The fund that invests Canada Pension Plan savings. It is joining a round led by General Catalyst and HarbourVest.
